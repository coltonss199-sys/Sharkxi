# Rhyme Pairs That Mean Something

1000 rhyme pairs where the sound link reinforces a meaning link, mined from the [CMU Pronouncing Dictionary](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict). There are 100 per vowel, from one search agent for each long and short vowel.

**`validate.py` checked every pair against CMU:**

- no r- or l-controlled syllables in either word (no ER, no vowel closed by R or L). An L or R that starts a syllable is fine (*bright*, *glow*, *delight*);
- the first word carries primary stress on the section's vowel;
- the rhyme type is the one CMU's phonemes actually support: **perfect** (identical from the stressed vowel on), **slant** (one or two near-consonant swaps, or a different vowel over identical endings), or **assonance** (same stressed vowel only);
- the relationship is 3-5 words and the example line uses both words.

The agents made the editorial calls: the meaning links, keeping words positive, and ranking (strongest sound and sense first). Each section ends with five near-misses that were rejected.

**Near-misses rejected (Long A)**

1. **celebration / jubilation**: Perfect rhyme and tight synonym, but CMU gives celebration an L-closed short vowel (S EH2 L), so it fails the r/l rule.
2. **ballet / sway**: Perfect rhyme and a vivid dance scene, but the spelling ball- reads as an L-controlled vowel under the updated spelling rule.
3. **plain / ornate**: Strong antonym with a long-A assonance, but the or- in ornate is an r-controlled syllable.
4. **fame / shame**: Perfect antonym rhyme, but shame is a negative, gloomy word the brief asks us to avoid.
5. **lemonade / shade**: Lovely summer scene, but CMU puts primary stress on lem- (EH), so lemonade is not stressed on long A.
